SECTION 1 — WHAT DAILYFORGE ACTUALLY SELLS
================================================================================

The product's value proposition is anti-feature:

    "One set of exercises. Every day. No edits, no excuses."

You are not selling more features. You are selling commitment enforcement.
The voice oath, the immutable exercise lock, the streak freeze — these are
friction intentionally designed to make quitting hard. That IS the product.

DIRECT IMPLICATION FOR THE PAYWALL:

    You cannot paywall the commitment mechanics.

If the voice oath, the daily seal, or the streak fall behind a paywall, the
app becomes a weaker product for free users and the whole identity collapses.
The core loop must stay free forever.

What CAN be paywalled:
    - Depth (analytics, insights)
    - Personalization (themes, more exercises)
    - Convenience (multiple reminders, unlimited freezes)

What CANNOT be paywalled:
    - The seal itself
    - The streak
    - Basic history
    - Basic reminders

================================================================================
SECTION 3 — PRICING
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL: YEARLY ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rationale for no monthly tier:
    - Yearly plans retain at 83.4% renewal vs. 39.2% for monthly.
    - Annual subscribers churn 2-3x less than monthly subscribers.
    - Two-plan paywalls (yearly + lifetime, or yearly alone) dominate at
      41-60% of top apps.
    - Adding monthly for this usage pattern only adds churn surface.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRICE: $39.99/YEAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reasoning:
    - Health & fitness yearly plans cluster at $29.99-$79.99 for freemium.
      (Strava $79.99, Fitbod $95.99, Cronometer $49.99)
    - Habit trackers specifically sit lower: $12.99-$44.99 yearly.
    - DailyForge is a habit tracker with a fitness surface, not a full
      fitness platform. Price in the habit band, not the Strava band.
    - $39.99 lands at the upper-middle of the habit band — serious enough
      to signal value, low enough that a committed user doesn't blink.

Why NOT $29.99:
    - Higher-priced apps convert paying users at ~2x the rate of cheap
      ones (9.8% vs. 4.3%).
    - Underpricing is more dangerous than overpricing for a first app.
    - $2.99/month signals "even the founder doesn't think this is worth
      much."

Why NOT $49.99+:
    - You'd compete with apps offering video content, adaptive AI
      programming, or nutrition databases. DailyForge doesn't.
    - Price what you deliver.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTIONAL: LIFETIME ANCHOR AT $79.99
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Data:
    - One-time purchases and lifetime plans grew from 6.4% to 10.3% of
      plan types between 2023 and 2025.

Benefits:
    - Serves users with subscription fatigue
    - Acts as an anchor making $39.99/year look cheap
    - Caps exposure to a small group who'd never subscribe anyway

Recommendation: OPTIONAL. Yearly alone is defensible. If A/B testing a
second price point, lifetime is the more interesting lever than monthly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIAL: 7-DAY FREE, NO CARD REQUIRED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rationale:
    - Data shows trials do NOT create stickier users (freemium and hard-
      paywall year-one retention is nearly identical).
    - But trials DO increase conversion.
    - DailyForge's loop is fast: one week of daily seals is enough to
      feel the streak working.
    - Health/wellness category recommends 14-day minimum, but DailyForge's
      loop is faster than most.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HARD PAYWALL VS. FREEMIUM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Numbers:
    - Hard paywall converts at 12.1%
    - Freemium converts at 2.2%
    - Hard paywall refund rates are 1.7x higher
    - Hard-paywall apps above 4.2% D35 conversion are rare

DECISION: FREEMIUM WITH A SOFT PAYWALL.
    - The free tier must be genuinely useful.
    - Pro is offered at natural limitation moments (see Section 5).

SECTION 4 — AD STRATEGY
================================================================================

OVERARCHING PRINCIPLE:

    Ads are the baseline monetization for free users, not the primary
    revenue. Pro subscription is the primary revenue. Ads capture the
    long tail of users who will never subscribe — and give Pro a concrete,
    immediately-felt benefit ("no more ads").

    The 2026 consensus: over 60% of top-grossing apps combine multiple
    revenue streams. Hybrid is the standard.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE NOT TO PUT ADS (CRITICAL LIST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Location                                  | Why not
------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------
During a session timer                    | Ruins the workout. User is mid-exercise.
                                          |
On the swear modal                        | The oath is sacred. An ad here is a
                                          | betrayal of the product's identity.
                                          |
On the celebration screen                 | The payoff moment. Never interrupt it.
                                          |
Between the seal and the streak update    | Same reason.
                                          |
On Today screen while exercises pending   | User is here to work. Don't distract.
                                          |
Interstitial after completing an exercise | The completion flow is 3 taps. An
                                          | interstitial between them is hostile.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE ADS BELONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PLACEMENT 1: Banner at the bottom of the History tab
-----------------------------------------------------

Why this works:
    - History is read-only, non-time-sensitive
    - Users spend 5-15 seconds there at most
    - No competing interest on the screen

Layout:
    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │  History header     │
    │  streak 12/30       │
    │  [This week] button │
    │                     │
    │  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○      │  <- 30-day grid
    │  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○      │
    │  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○      │
    │  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○      │
    │                     │
    │  [  banner ad  ]    │  <- bottom, scrolls with content
    └─────────────────────┘

Format: 320x50 adaptive banner
Position: Bottom of scroll content (scrolls with content, not fixed)

PLACEMENT 2: Interstitial after the weekly recap is dismissed
--------------------------------------------------------------

Why this works:
    - User explicitly opened the recap modal
    - Dismissing returns to History (a transition moment)
    - Standard, expected pattern

Frequency cap:
    - MAX once per day
    - NEVER if the user opened the recap in the last 24 hours
    - NEVER two in a row

Flow:
    Weekly Recap modal
           ↓
      User taps "Close"
           ↓
    [ Interstitial appears ]     <- once per day max
           ↓
      History screen

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AD FORMAT RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Format           | Use                                    | Frequency
-----------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------
Adaptive banner  | History bottom                         | Always visible there
                 |                                        |
Interstitial     | After weekly recap dismiss             | Max 1/day, never 2x
                 |                                        |
Rewarded         | "Watch ad to freeze a missed day"      | User-initiated only
                 | (SEE WARNING BELOW)                    |
                 |                                        |
App open         | NO                                     | Never — poison

APP OPEN ADS — SPECIAL WARNING:
    They generate good revenue because every session starts with one.
    They also produce the highest uninstall rate of any ad format.
    For an app whose value is "open it daily and feel good," a cold-start
    ad is poison. DO NOT USE.

REWARDED ADS — WARNING:
    Concept "watch a 30-second ad to earn a streak freeze" is compelling:
    opt-in, gives real value, monetizes 3-5x better than banners.
    BUT it cheapens the freeze mechanic. If free users can earn unlimited
    freezes by watching ads, the freeze stops meaning anything.
    RECOMMENDATION: SKIP for now. Freezes should be earned by
    consistency, not by watching videos.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AD SDK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use: react-native-google-mobile-ads
    - Maintained community fork of AdMob's RN SDK
    - Regularly updated by Google
    - Better mediation support than expo-ads-admob
    - Works with Expo bare workflow and EAS builds

Do NOT use: expo-ads-admob
    - Fewer advanced options
    - Updates less frequently

START WITH ADMOB ONLY:
    Once you have traffic data, layer in Meta Audience Network or
    AppLovin via mediation. Starting with mediation before you have
    volume is premature optimization.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REALISTIC AD REVENUE EXPECTATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Banner eCPMs (US-heavy fitness audience):     $2-$6 per 1,000 impressions
Interstitial eCPMs:                            $8-$15

If a free user opens the app daily and views one banner + one interstitial:

    Per user per day:      ~$0.005-$0.012
    Per user per year:     ~$1.80-$4.40

Compare to Pro:
    $39.99/year for one Pro subscriber
    = 10-20 free ad-supported users

This is why ads are the floor, not the strategy. Pro is where the
business lives.

SECTION 5 — PAYWALL PLACEMENT AND UX
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHEN TO SHOW THE PAYWALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NOT on first launch
NOT during onboarding
NOT on the Today screen unprompted

SHOW IT AT MOMENTS OF NATURAL LIMITATION:

    1. User taps "Add Exercise" and already has 3-5
       -> Soft sheet: "You've reached the free limit. Pro unlocks
          unlimited exercises."
          Buttons: "Upgrade" / "Maybe later"

    2. User opens advanced analytics screen (if it exists)
       -> Free users see a preview with blurred/teased overlay

    3. User taps "Remove Ads" from Settings
       -> Self-selected path. Highest intent.

    4. After the 7th day of consecutive streaks
       -> Milestone moment: "You've sealed 7 days straight. Pro keeps
          the streak alive with unlimited freezes."

NEVER SHOW THE PAYWALL ON:
    - The swear modal
    - The celebration screen
    - During a session

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAYWALL SCREEN REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three things it needs:

    1. The yearly price, clearly labeled as the only option.
       "$39.99/year" — no fake "was $79.99" strike-through.
       No dark patterns.

    2. The trial: "7 days free. Cancel anytime."
       No card required means the trial starts automatically when they
       tap "Start Free Trial."

    3. A single benefit list. FOUR BULLETS MAX. Everything else is noise.

Design language should match the app: clean, no guilt, no manipulation.

POSITIONING NOTE:
    The Habit Tracker Kit explicitly markets itself as "no streak shame,
    no dark patterns" — that's exactly the positioning DailyForge should
    claim.

SECTION 6 — RISKS AND HONEST CONCERNS
================================================================================

RISK 1: Ads damage the "serious, no-BS" brand
------------------------------------------------
DailyForge positions itself as an anti-frivolous app. Ads are inherently
frivolous. A banner on the History screen is a visible compromise.

If brand damage outweighs the ~$2-$4/year per free user, drop ads entirely
and go pure freemium. The subscription revenue is what matters.

RISK 2: The 3-exercise cap may be wrong for this app
--------------------------------------------------------
DailyForge's premise is "you set your daily routine and commit." A user
might genuinely want 5 exercises from day one. Capping at 3 might force
an upgrade before the user has felt the value.

Consider: cap at 5 instead of 3.
    - Costs some upgrade pressure
    - Makes the free tier a complete product for most users
    - Upsell becomes "unlimited + analytics + themes" rather than
      "unlimited vs. limited"

RISK 3: Premium adoption shows a burst that fades
------------------------------------------------------
Research from Marketing Science found that premium adoption in mHealth
apps generates an immediate engagement lift that ATTENUATES within several
weeks. It does NOT translate into sustained behavior change.

Paying for an app doesn't make people stick with it.

Implication: Pro features need to be ones users keep using. Advanced
analytics and unlimited exercises fit. Cosmetic themes do not.

RISK 4: "Free tier is too good" -> nobody upgrades
--------------------------------------------------------
The opposite of Risk 2. If the free tier gives everything a user needs,
conversion sits at freemium's 2.2% floor.

The balance: free must be COMPLETE but not SATISFYING — the user should
feel the ceiling without feeling cheated.

SECTION 7 — OPEN QUESTIONS / DECISIONS NEEDED
================================================================================

1. FREE EXERCISE CAP: 3 or 5?

   Recommendation: 5.
   Makes the free tier a genuinely usable product. Upsell pressure comes
   from analytics and themes instead of the exercise cap.

2. ADS: YES OR NO?

   Recommendation: Yes, but only the two placements above, and only if
   comfortable with the brand trade-off.

3. LIFETIME PRICE POINT: ADD ONE OR NOT?

   Recommendation: Yes at $79.99 as an anchor, even if nobody buys it.
   Makes $39.99/year look right.

4. WHICH PRO FEATURES ARE BUILDABLE IN THE NEXT MONTH?

   - Unlimited exercises: trivial (remove a cap)
   - Themes: trivial (unlock existing color schemes)
   - Advanced analytics: real work (new queries, new charts)
   - Multi-reminder: scheduler changes

   Which of these are worth the build time?

5. HARD TRIAL OR OPT-IN TRIAL?

   Hard:   auto-starts when they tap "Start Free Trial" and charges
           after 7 days unless cancelled. Converts better.
   Opt-in: they explicitly activate the trial. Fewer refunds.
